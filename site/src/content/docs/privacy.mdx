This page describes how the hosted service at **app.silencewatch.com** handles your personal data, in line with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the French data-protection law. It does not apply to an instance you host yourself: in that case the publisher has access to nothing.

## Who is responsible

The controller is the publisher of the service, whose details are in the [legal notice](/legal/). For any question or request: <strong>{LEGAL.email}</strong>.

## The data, and why

| Data | Why | How long it is kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Account**: email address, and what your way of signing in needs: the public key of each passkey (never the private one), a password hash (scrypt, never the password itself), or the identifier your Google or Microsoft account has with us, with recovery codes as hashes. No profile picture and no token of those providers is kept | To create and run your account | As long as the account exists |
| **Sessions**: a session token, and the IP address and browser of the sign-in | To keep you signed in and to detect a stolen session | 30 days from the last time it was used, and until you sign out |
| **Security log**: sensitive actions (sign-in, password change, API keys, deletions…), with the email, IP address and browser | Security of the service and investigation after an incident | 1 year. It keeps the email of whoever acted even after the account is deleted |
| **Sign-up**: the network the request came from, truncated (IPv4 /24, IPv6 /48) | To slow automated sign-ups | 7 days |
| **Your configuration**: projects, checks (names, schedules, environments, tags), alert channels (email addresses, webhook addresses, Slack, Teams, Discord), API keys (as a hash) | To provide the service | As long as you keep them |
| **Heartbeat history**: time, result, duration, the caller’s IP address and browser, and any text attached (10,000 bytes at most) | To detect that a job has stopped and to show the history | 90 days by default, adjustable per project |
| **Alerts sent**: recipient, time, outcome | To tell you, and to know whether the alert went out | 30 days |

Do not send third parties’ personal data in the text attached to a heartbeat: it is stored as it is with the history.

The service uses **no audience-measurement tool, no advertising and no tracker**, and neither sells nor rents any data.

## Legal bases

- **Performance of the contract** (GDPR article 6.1.b) for everything that makes the account and the service work.
- **Legitimate interest** (article 6.1.f) for security: rate limiting, the security log, and fighting automated sign-ups.
- **A legal obligation** (article 6.1.c) where the law requires information to be kept or disclosed.

## Who the data is shared with

Data is shared only with the providers the service needs, as processors:

- **OVH SAS** (France): hosting of the servers and the database.
- **Google and Microsoft**, only if you choose to sign in with them: they tell us your email address and an identifier, and they learn that you sign in to SilenceWatch. We receive no profile picture and keep none of their tokens.
- **Mailjet**: sending the alert emails you have asked for. The service sends no other email: nothing is sent to confirm an address or to reset a password.

OVH and Mailjet are established in the European Union. Every provider receives only what its task requires. The alerts you configure towards Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord or a webhook of your choosing are sent to the service you designated yourself, under your responsibility.

## Your rights

You may ask for **access** to your data, its **rectification**, its **erasure**, the **restriction** of its processing, its **portability**, and you may **object** to processing based on legitimate interest. Write to <strong>{LEGAL.email}</strong> giving your account’s address; you will get an answer within one month.

You can delete your account yourself, in **Settings → Account → Delete account**: deletion is immediate and permanent, and takes your projects, checks, their history, API keys and alert channels with it. Only the entries in the security log are kept, for the period given above.

If you think your rights are not respected, you may complain to the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/complaints), the French data-protection authority, or to the authority of your own country.

## Cookies and browser storage

The application sets **one cookie that lasts**, `sw.session_token`: the one that keeps you signed in. It is `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Secure`, and essential to the service. While you sign in it also sets a few cookies that live for minutes (the passkey challenge, the handshake with Google or Microsoft). As such none of them needs your consent.

It also stores your display preferences (language, light or dark mode) and the selected project in your browser’s local storage. These values stay on your device and are never sent to the server.

The silencewatch.com site sets no cookie; its display theme is also kept locally.

## Security

Passwords, where they are used, are stored as scrypt hashes, and recovery codes and API keys as hashes too. Passkeys are public keys, from which no one can sign in. The session cookie is signed with a secret the database does not hold, and traffic is encrypted (HTTPS). The measures are detailed on the [Security](/docs/reference/security/) page. A vulnerability is reported through the procedure in [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/liliang-dev/SilenceWatch/blob/main/SECURITY.md).
