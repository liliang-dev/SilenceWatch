<p class="splash-lede">In a Laravel application, the whole schedule runs from a single <code>schedule:run</code> every minute. If it stops, or one task stops, nothing tells you. The <strong>SilenceWatch package</strong> reads your schedule, declares every task with its real cron expression, and reports every run.</p>

## One package, one key

```bash
composer require silencewatch/laravel
```

```dotenv
SILENCEWATCH_API_KEY=sw_...
```

That is the whole setup: Laravel discovers the package, and nothing in your schedule changes. The first time `schedule:run` runs a task, the whole schedule is declared in SilenceWatch with its real cron expressions, and each task sends a heartbeat around every run, with its duration and its failures. No check to create by hand, no ping URL to copy, nothing to keep in sync when you edit `routes/console.php`.

## What sets it apart

- **It listens, it does not wrap.** The package uses the events Laravel's scheduler already fires, so your tasks are not changed, and background tasks (`->runInBackground()`) are reported with their exit code.
- **It reads the real schedules**, with each task's own time zone (`->timezone()`), else the application's.
- **It gives each task a stable identity** (`artisan:backup:run --only-db`, `exec:/opt/scripts/export.sh`, `call:App\Jobs\PruneOrders`): history survives deploys, renames and moves between servers.
- **It never deletes anything.** A task that leaves the schedule is flagged *orphaned*; the history is kept until you delete it.
- **It tells environments apart**, with `APP_ENV` by default: staging and production are two separate sets of checks.
- **It tells you what it does not watch.** `php artisan silencewatch:sync` declares the schedule at the end of a deploy script and lists the tasks left out, with the reason.

## It cannot hurt you

- it **never fails** your task: every error is caught, and a task that succeeds is never reported to Laravel as failed because of the package;
- it **costs almost nothing**: PHP has no background thread, so a heartbeat is a request the scheduler waits for, at most two seconds, and once SilenceWatch has not answered, the other tasks of that run do not wait for it again;
- it **degrades silently**: with no key, no network or no server, the schedule runs exactly as without the package;
- `SILENCEWATCH_ENABLED=false` **removes the whole mechanism**;
- it has **no dependency** beyond Laravel itself. Laravel 12 and 13, PHP 8.2 or later.

All settings and limits are in the [package documentation](/docs/laravel/).

## If your job is not in the Laravel scheduler

Any job that can make an HTTP request can be monitored with the [ping API](/docs/ping-api/). See [cron job monitoring](/cron-monitoring/) and the [examples](/docs/cron-examples/).
